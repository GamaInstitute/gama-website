## Before you start {#intro}

Our goal is to make research accessible while helping contributors develop their understanding, presentation skills and confidence.

Undergraduate students and first-time presenters are welcome. You do not need to be an expert, but you should understand the paper well enough to explain its main ideas accurately.

## 1. Choose a research paper {#article}

Select a paper that interests you and offers useful insights for the GAMA community.

- Note its title, authors and link.
- Focus on one main paper, using additional sources when helpful.

No paper in mind yet? The [Finding research papers](/guides/finding-papers) guide explains how to search ACM Digital Library and arXiv.

> If you have difficulty accessing or downloading the paper, [write to us](mailto:contact@gamainstitute.ca) and we will help.

## 2. Download the presentation template {#modele}

Download the [GAMA PowerPoint template](download:slide_template) and use it to prepare your slides. Follow its visual identity while adapting layouts, diagrams and tables to explain your paper clearly.

## 3. Follow the GAMA structure {#structure}

Organize your presentation around the following sections. You may also suggest an innovative structure, as long as it explains the research clearly.

| Section | What to include |
|---|---|
| Intro | Introduce a realistic situation or engaging question. Present the paper and explain why the problem matters. |
| Key concepts | Explain the essential terms and ideas the audience needs to understand the research. |
| Methodology | Describe what the researchers did and how they evaluated their approach, without unnecessary technical detail. |
| Results | Present the most important findings and explain what they mean. |
| Discussion & implications | Discuss practical lessons, important limitations and possible future directions. Distinguish your interpretation from the authors' conclusions. |
| Question to the audience | End with an accessible, open question that encourages viewers to share their experiences or perspectives. |

## 4. Language and slide design {#langue}

The presentation is intended for a **French-speaking audience**:

- Deliver your presentation **in French**.
- Write slide titles and content **in English**, including if you are a native French speaker.
- Explain technical terms and abbreviations in accessible language.
- Keep slides concise, with one main message per slide.

You may create your own images, diagrams or tables to explain concepts or summarize findings. Add source credits and clearly label illustrative examples. Credit any reused or adapted visuals and check their reuse conditions.

## 5. Record your presentation {#enregistrer}

Record a video of your presentation. You may follow the style of [GAMA WeekPaper videos](/week-paper) or choose another format that communicates the research clearly.

**You may record with or without showing your face.** For example, record your slides with voice narration, appear alongside your slides, or combine these formats.

Make sure your voice is clear, your slides are readable and the recording is easy to follow.

## 6. Prepare the publication materials {#publication}

Along with your presentation, prepare:

- **A proposed YouTube title:** clear, engaging and faithful to the research.
- **A YouTube description in French:** introduce the topic, explain what viewers will learn, and include the paper reference and link.
- **A profile photo:** it identifies you in the video thumbnail, including when your face does not appear in the recording.
- **A brief bio:** a few sentences introducing your background and interests.
- **Your LinkedIn profile link, if you have one:** to credit you and help viewers discover your work.

Browse the existing videos on the [GAMA Institute YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@gamainstitut) for examples of titles and descriptions.

## 7. Choose your publication preference {#diffusion}

When submitting, select one option:

- **YouTube and LinkedIn:** GAMA publishes the video on its YouTube channel and shares or promotes your contribution on its LinkedIn page.
- **YouTube only:** GAMA publishes the video on its YouTube channel, without sharing or promoting it on LinkedIn.

## 8. Submit your contribution {#soumettre}

Use the [submission form on the WeekPaper page](/week-paper#contribute). It asks for:

- a link to your presentation video (Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox or WeTransfer);
- your editable PowerPoint slides;
- the original paper's reference and link;
- your proposed YouTube title and description;
- your full name, profile photo, brief bio and, if you have one, your LinkedIn profile link;
- your publication preference.

## 9. Accuracy, review and publication {#revision}

Explain the research in your own words, verify numerical results, acknowledge important limitations and credit your sources.

GAMA Institute reviews every submission for clarity, scientific accuracy, accessibility and alignment with these guidelines. Published contributions credit the presenters and acknowledge the original research authors, and are released under a CC BY 4.0 licence (see our [Open Access Policy](/open-access)).

> **Before submitting:** can a French-speaking viewer unfamiliar with the paper understand its problem, approach, main findings and practical significance?
